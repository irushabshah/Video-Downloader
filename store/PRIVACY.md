# Yoink privacy policy

Last updated: November 3, 2026

Yoink is a Chrome extension that saves videos and audio to your computer. This page says what it touches and where that goes.

## The short version

Everything stays on your computer. Yoink has no servers and no accounts. I don't collect, sell or share your data.

## What Yoink reads

Yoink reads the address and title of the tab you're on. It uses them to fill in the link and to name your file.

Yoink watches the network responses of the pages you visit, to spot video files and streams. It only looks at the headers that say what kind of file a response is and how big it is. It keeps this list in your browser until you close the tab or leave the page.

When "Use my logins" is on, Yoink reads the cookies for the link you download. This lets you download videos you can see when you're signed in. You can turn it off in the popup under Settings.

## Where it goes

All of it goes only to the Yoink helper, a program you install on your own computer. The helper uses it to run the download, then forgets it. Cookies go into a temporary file that only your user account can read. The helper deletes that file as soon as the download ends.

To check a stream's size, Yoink fetches the stream's playlist from the site that serves it. Sometimes it also asks for the size of a few pieces of the stream. Your browser makes the same kind of requests when you play the video.

Nothing is sent to me or to anyone else. Yoink has no analytics and no tracking.

## What Yoink stores

Yoink stores your save folder, your settings and a list of your last 20 downloads in Chrome's storage on your computer. You can remove items from the list in the popup. Removing the extension removes all of it.

If this policy changes, I'll update this page and the date at the top.
